This detailed mapping outlines the theoretical framework and conceptual terrain of *Feminist, Queer, Crip*, presenting concepts in normalized (lowercase) form alongside their associated authors as requested.

### Introduction: Imagined Futures

* **Defining disability: a political/relational model**  
* **individual model of disability**: Frames disability as a personal, pathological problem residing within the individual that requires strength of character to "overcome" 1-3.  
* **medical model of disability**: Positions atypical bodies and minds as deviant and defective, treating the condition itself as objective fact and common sense 2, 4, 5\.  
* **common sense logic / objective fact**: The positioning of medicalized meanings as facts beyond debate or dissent 4, 6, 7\. **Author: Denis Dutton (critiqued by Kafer)** 6-8.  
* **social model of disability**: Recasts disability in social terms, distinguishing between **impairment** (physical/mental limitation) and **disability** (social exclusion/barriers) 9, 10\.  
* **impairment/disability distinction**: A binary where impairment is physical and disability is social; Kafer critiques this for failing to recognize that both are socially constructed and for erasing the lived reality of pain 10-12.  
* **political/relational model of disability**: Kafer’s core framework; it treats disability as a product of social patterns and built environments, viewing medical diagnoses as ideological and political sites 10, 13, 14\. **Author: Alison Kafer** 13\.  
* **hierarchical division of bodies and minds**: A binary used to legitimate unequal resource distribution and power 9\. **Author: Rosemarie Garland-Thomson** 9, 15\.  
* **the political**: A dimension inherent to every society determined by contested power relations 16\. **Author: Chantal Mouffe** 16, 17\.  
* **the how of politics / depoliticization**: The process through which concepts are rendered "common sense" to block them from political circulation or agendas 18, 19\. **Author: Jodi Dean** 17-19.  
* **cultural imagination vs. personal imagination**: A distinction regarding simulation exercises that often lack the voices of disabled people 20\. **Author: Tobin Siebers** 5, 20\.  
* **identifying disability: bodies, identities, politics**  
* **assemblage**: Identities (race, gender, disability) understood as events, actions, and encounters rather than fixed attributes 21\. **Author: Jasbir Puar** 21, 22\.  
* **collective affinity**: Identifications based on social/political circumstances of exclusion rather than essential biology 23, 24\. **Author: Joan W. Scott (drawing on Donna Haraway)** 22, 23\.  
* **the "disabled we"**: A community bound by shared social circumstances rather than collective symptoms; described as "promise than fact" 24-26. **Author: Simi Linton** 22, 25\.  
* **nondisabled claim to crip**: A practice of solidarity that refuses the sick/healthy binary while risking appropriation 24, 27\. **Authors: Robert McRuer, Carrie Sandahl** 24, 28\.  
* **crip affiliation**: The framework of practices and institutions that structure how all people create a way of life, regardless of identity 29\. **Author: Ladelle McWhorter** 29, 30\.  
* **Feminist, crip, queer: a note on terms, methods, and affiliations**  
* **crip theory**: A contestatory theoretical project exploring the risks of identity politics while recognizing its generative role in rights movements 31\. **Authors: Robert McRuer, Carrie Sandahl** 31, 32\.  
* **crip / cripple as inducement to wince**: The intentional use of "crip" to jolt people out of normative understandings 33\. **Author: Nancy Mairs** 33, 34\.  
* **words to help forge a politics**: The use of "queer" and "crip" as political tools 33\. **Author: Eli Clare** 32, 33\.  
* **compulsory able-bodiedness**: A system requiring normative physical functioning, intertwined with heteronormativity 35, 36\. **Author: Robert McRuer** 17, 35\.  
* **compulsory able-mindedness**: A system requiring normative mental and cognitive processes 35, 36\. **Author: Alison Kafer** 35, 37\.  
* **queer as site of collective contestation**: Queerness as a term to be constantly redeployed and "twisted" rather than stabilized 38\. **Author: Judith Butler** 38, 39\.  
* **coalitional politics / coalitional methodology**: A methodology that reads disability into narratives that may not explicitly name it 40, 41\.  
* **Overview of the book**  
* **crip futurity**: A politics of engagement that yearns for an **"elsewhere/elsewhen"** where disability is understood as political and valuable 42-44. **Author: Alison Kafer** 42, 45\.

### Chapter 1: Time for Disability Studies and a Future for Crips

* **no future for crips**  
* **reproductive futurism**: A political logic where **"the Child"** is the beneficiary of every political intervention, almost always imagined as able-bodied 46, 47\. **Author: Lee Edelman (drawing on Lauren Berlant)** 46, 48\.  
* **ethics of endless deferral**: Pouring resources into "curing" future people while ignoring the needs of the present 49\. **Author: Lee Edelman** 49, 50\.  
* **three generations of imbeciles are enough**: A eugenicist slogan from legal history justifying sterilization 3\. **Author: Oliver Wendell Holmes** 3, 50\.  
* **queer / racialized children out of futurity**: The observation that non-white and queer children are not the "sovereign princes" of reproductive futurism 51, 52\. **Author: José Esteban Muñoz** 51, 53\.  
* **whiteness of health and hygiene**: Historical linkages among race, class, and hygiene standards 54, 55\. **Author: Anna Stubblefield** 54, 55\.  
* **tangle of pathology**: A racialized framing of marginalized families as inherently sick 52\. **Author: Daniel Patrick Moynihan** 52, 56\.  
* **biopolitics of regenerative capacity**: Mapping how populations are marked for "no future" based on their inability to regenerate 57\. **Author: Jasbir Puar** 57, 58\.  
* **dead in jail or in a chair**: An intersectional framing of "no future" for black men in certain urban environments 59\. **Author: Noam Ostrander** 58, 59\.  
* **Queer time, crip time**  
* **crip time** (community-based): A wry reference to disability-related events starting late or the "extra time" needed for tasks 60, 61\. **Authors: Irv Zola, Carol Gill** 60, 62\.  
* **crip time** (flexibility): A reorientation to time that recognizes different rates of processing and movement; "flex time exploded" 63\. **Author: Margaret Price** 63, 64\.  
* **curative time / curative imaginary**: A temporality casting disabled people as "out of time" or as obstacles to progress until cured 65, 66\. **Author: Alison Kafer** 65\.  
* **time is out of joint**: A link between skeletal dislocation and temporal dissonance 67\. **Author: Elizabeth Freeman (riffing on Shakespeare)** 58, 67\.  
* **time of coincidence**: A temporality where cycles of time "fall" together rather than passing linearly 68\. **Author: Tom Boellstorff** 68, 69\.  
* **falling as moment of identification**: Tripping as a moment of category confusion between "passing" and "disabled" 70\. **Author: Eliza Chandler** 69, 70\.  
* **prognosis time**: A liminal temporality where a diagnosis replaces the future with a "short future" 71, 72\. **Author: Sarah Lochlann Jain** 69, 71\.  
* **undiagnosis time**: The temporal experience of shuttling between specialists without recognition 73\.  
* **time of the repeated attempt**: An orientation defined by the repeated experience of being denied recognition or services 73\. **Author: Christopher Nealon** 73, 74\.  
* **anticipatory time**: Scanning the environment for past triggers (like chemical exposures) to survive the immediate future 75\. **Author: Mel Chen** 75, 76\.  
* **imaginative life schedules**: The simultaneous inhabiting of present and future required to manage things like attendant care 77-79.  
* **eccentric economic practices**: Living outside capital accumulation through barter systems or disability-related survival strategies 80, 81\. **Author: Judith Halberstam** 81-83.  
* **On longevity, lost history, and futurity**  
* **critique of longevity**: Challenging long life as the only desirable future; noting that **"surviving well can mean surviving sick"** 84-86. **Author: Judith Halberstam** 84 (critiqued by Kafer 86, 87).  
* **reformulated histories**: Contesting meanings of the past to forge new practices and pleasures 88\. **Author: Shannon Winnubst** 88, 89\.  
* **writing letters to the dead**: A method of contesting disability history and refusing boundaries of place/time 88\. **Authors: Georgina Kleege, Brenda Brueggemann** 88, 89\.  
* **queer utopian memory**: A memory that contests the past to forge new practices, distinct from standard nostalgia 90\. **Author: José Esteban Muñoz** 90, 91\.  
* **compulsory nostalgia**: A requirement that disabled people long for a "lost" nondisabled past self that may never have existed 90, 92, 93\. **Author: Alison Kafer** 90\.  
* **time of incarceration / institutionalization**: A temporality defined by "doing time" or being "permanently placed" 94, 95\.  
* **Future desires, present despair**  
* **lust born of recognition**: The desire to see bodies like one’s own in the future 96\. **Authors: Mia Mingus, Leah Lakshmi Piepzna-Samarasinha** 96, 97\.  
* **erotically invested affirmation**: The felt need for queer/disabled lives to be treated as a "precious desideratum" 98, 99\. **Author: Eve Kosofsky Sedgwick** 98, 100\.  
* **fantasy as critical promise**: Using fantasy to imagine oneself and others "otherwise" 101\. **Author: Judith Butler** 100, 101\.  
* **hope inseparable from despair**: The intensity of yearning for crip futures born from their current absence 101\. **Author: Heather Love** 100, 101\.

### Chapter 2: At the Same Time, Out of Time: Ashley X

* **A case history of the ashley treatment**  
* **the ashley treatment**: Growth attenuation using high-dose estrogen, combined with a hysterectomy and bilateral mastectomy 102-104. **Authors: Daniel Gunther, Douglas Diekema** 105, 106\.  
* **Documenting the ashley treatment**  
* **pillow angel**: A term infantilizing profoundly disabled children to align their bodies with perceived cognitive infancy 104, 107, 108\. **Authors: Ashley's Parents** 107, 109\.  
* **Out of line, out of time**  
* **embodied asynchrony**: The framing of a child whose mind and body are seen as "growing apart" 102, 110-112. **Authors: Daniel Gunther, Douglas Diekema** 106, 112\.  
* **adulthood as productivity**: ad Adulthood defined by independence and labor; disability renders this "unachievable" 113-115.  
* **cadillac with a chevrolet engine**: A bioethical metaphor positioning the disabled adult as a "defective product" 115\. **Author: Norman Fost** 115, 116\.  
* **the grotesque / matter out of place**: The "jarring" disconnect of seeing an adult body with a baby's mind 117, 118\. **Authors: Mary Douglas (concept)** 118, 119, **Norman Fost (application)** 116, 117\.  
* **reproductive use-value**: The assumption that female organs only have value for reproduction; if not "useful," they are expendable 120, 121\.  
* **“Towards a better quality of life for ‘pillow angels’”**  
* **template for the future**: The hope that growth attenuation becomes a standard for other families 110, 117, 122\. **Authors: Ashley's Parents** 122, 123\.  
* **permissibility guidelines**: Establishing "nonambulatory" and "noncommunicative" as moral criteria 124, 125\. **Author: Seattle Growth Attenuation and Ethics Working Group (SWG)** 124-126.  
* **The future will be privatized: the ashley case in context**  
* **privatized future**: Casting disability and caregiving as private familial problems rather than public policy 127-129.  
* **"god trick" of objectivity**: The belief that medical bioethicists act without political bias, unlike advocates 130\. **Author: Donna Haraway (applied by Douglas Diekema)** 130, 131\.  
* **disability gulag**: Term describing the institutionalization and segregation of disabled people 132\. **Author: Harriet McBryde Johnson** 132, 133\.  
* **Unknown futures, narrowed futures: measuring “quality of life”**  
* **quality of life** (as common sense): An assumed self-evident measure based on function and pain 134\.  
* **expert underestimation**: The history of professionals underestimating the cognitive potential of the "profoundly impaired" 135\. **Authors: Adrienne Asch, Anna Stubblefield** 135, 136\.  
* **foreclosure of pleasure**: The inability to imagine the disabled body as a source of pleasurable sensation rather than just pain 137, 138\.  
* **At the same time, out of time; or, looking for ashley among crips and queers**  
* **permanently unabled**: A category used to separate profoundly disabled children from the larger movement 139, 140\. **Authors: Ashley's Parents** 139, 141\.

### Chapter 3: Debating Feminist Futures

* **This is what the future looks like: reproduction and debate in woman on the edge of time**  
* **feminist utopia as disability-free**: Utopian visions where congenital disability is engineered out of existence while diversity is otherwise celebrated 142-145. **Author: Marge Piercy (novel)** 142, 146; **José van Dijck (critique)** 147, 148\.  
* **erasure of difference**: Achieving communal harmony by technically erasing reproductive or racial differences 145\.  
* **Deaf/disabled: a terminological interlude**  
* **linguistic-cultural model of deafness**: Framing Deaf people as a linguistic minority with a unique culture rather than as disabled 149\. **Author: Harlan Lane** 149, 150\.  
* **everyone spoke sign language**: Historical models of shared responsibility for communication (e.g., Martha's Vineyard) 151\. **Author: Nora Groce** 151, 152\.  
* **Reproducing cultural anxiety: the case of the deaf lesbians**  
* **special blessing**: The intentional selection of a Deaf donor to have a Deaf child 153\. **Authors: Sharon Duchesneau, Candace McCullough** 153, 154\.  
* **slippery slope**: Dystopic rhetoric suggesting that "manufacturing" disabled children leads to a frightening future 155-157. **Authors: Ken Connor (Family Research Council)** 155, 158, **Jeanette Winterson** 156, 159\.  
* **reconsolidation of the nuclear family**: Reproductive technology used to reinforce white heterosexual norms by policing "nontraditional" users 160\. **Authors: Sarah Franklin, Laura Mamo** 160, 161\.  
* **expert surveillance**: Requiring disabled parents to prove "fitness" to medical experts 157, 162\. **Author: Carrie Lucas** 162, 163\.  
* **Open to debate? disability and difference in a feminist future**  
* **quality of life rhetoric**: A critique of using the term to mask dissent and ignore diverse desires 164\. **Author: Michael Warner** 164, 165\.  
* **disability as a source of knowledge**: Living with impairment provides unique perspectives on intimacy and interdependence 166, 167\. **Author: Susan Wendell** 166, 168\.

### Chapter 4: A Future for Whom? Billboard Liberation

* **Super man’s values and the quest for a better life**  
* **supercrip**: The media trope of disabled people "overcoming" their disabilities through individual character, used to inspire the nondisabled 169-171.  
* **gendered devotion**: Differential celebration of caregiving based on gender norms (e.g., fatherly vs. motherly help) 172\. **Author: Alison Kafer** 172\.  
* **A better life for whom? foundational foreclosures**  
* **individualist responsibility**: Framing disability as a character issue, removing it from structural inequality 173-176.  
* **extrapolitical**: Strategic use of the "disabled body" or "the Child" to present specific values as self-evident and beyond debate 177, 178\. **Authors: Lauren Berlant, Lee Edelman** 177-179.  
* **Queercrip futures**  
* **coalition values**: Defining community and "courage" through political protest and dissent rather than forced unity 180, 181\.  
* **guerrilla liberation / billboard liberation**: Acts of reclaiming media spaces (billboards) to turn disability into a site of resistance and desire 182-184.

### Chapter 5: The Cyborg and the Crip

* **“Rise of the cyborgs”**  
* **the cyborg manifesto**: A call for feminist politics based on "hybridity" and "affinity" rather than nature 185-189. **Author: Donna Haraway** 185\.  
* **kinship with animals and machines**: Blurring boundaries between machine and organism to guide us to a "more livable place" 185\. **Author: Donna Haraway** 185, 190\.  
* **Heroic “cyborg citizens”**  
* **cyborg citizen**: A posthuman subject navigating shifting identities and contingent alliances 191-193. **Author: Chris Hables Gray** 191, 194\.  
* **united front of invalid cyborgs**: A critique of reducing disabled people to their machines, resulting in apolitical unification 192, 193\. **Author: Chris Hables Gray** 192 (critiqued by Kafer 193).  
* **“paraplegics and other severely handicapped” cyborgs**  
* **intense experiences of complex hybridization**: Positioning disabled people as exemplary hybrids while ignoring their material realities 195-197. **Author: Donna Haraway** 195, 198\.  
* **imperializing narrative**: The risk of white feminists romanticizing or homogenizing others (Asian chip makers, disabled people) as repositories of wisdom 199-202. **Authors: Malini Johar Schueller, Joan W. Scott, Mariana Ortega** 199, 200, 202, 203\.  
* **Cyborg attachments**  
* **companion species**: A figure focusing on the co-constitutiveness of dogs and humans 204\. **Author: Donna Haraway** 204, 205\.  
* **replacement figures**: Successor tropes like the **vampire** or the **grotesque** 206\. **Authors: Ingrid Bartsch, Carolyn Dipalma, Laura Sells (vampire)** 205, 206; **Sara Cohen Shabot (grotesque)** 205, 206\.  
* **Pushing the cyborg: cripping cyborg politics**  
* **trans-species being-in-the-world**: The shared existence created between a human and a service animal 207, 208\. **Authors: Rod Michalko, Cary Wolfe** 207, 209\.  
* **combined unit**: The dual identity created between a disabled person and their personal attendant 208\. **Author: Loree Erickson** 208, 209\.  
* **subject position vs identity**: Seeing the cyborg as a position relative to production and insurance rather than just a physical interface 210\. **Author: Steven Kurzman** 210, 211\.  
* **"severe" as defiance**: Reinterpreting the word "severe" to mean fieriness and critique 212\. **Author: Robert McRuer** 212, 213\.  
* **ironic blasphemy**: Using ironic slogans (e.g., "trached dykes eat pussy") to turn technologies into sites of resistance and pleasure 214-221. **Authors: Donna Haraway** 214, **Connie Panzarino** 215, 222, **Michelle O'Brien** 220, 221, 223, **Dean Spade** 221, 223\.  
* **Cyborg histories, cyborg futures**  
* **cyborg of involuntary medication**: The original 1960 cyborg figure intended for space travel, drawing on institutionalized psychiatric patients 224-226. **Authors: Manfred Clynes, Nathan Kline** 224, 227\.

### Chapter 6: Bodies of Nature

* **Natural exclusions**  
* **industrial tourism**: The critique that technology (cars) alienates humans from "real" nature 228\. **Author: Edward Abbey** 228, 229\.  
* **disability-equals-alienation trope**: The use of disability as a symbol for the machine’s corruption of body-nature harmony 228, 230\. **Author: Sarah Jaquette Ray** 228, 231\.  
* **wilderness ideal body**: The construction of the outdoors as a proving ground for solo, unmediated bodies 232, 233\. **Author: Sarah Jaquette Ray** 231, 232\.  
* **hiking as authorizing gesture**: The assumption that physical immersion is the only way to attain environmental insight 230, 234\. **Author: Linda Vance** 234, 235\.  
* **Accessible trails and other (un)natural disasters**  
* **environmental injustice**: Thoughtless design that prevents disabled people from using nature 236, 237\. **Authors: Rob Imrie, Huw Thomas** 236, 238\.  
* **artifacts of access as "unnatural"**: Ramps seen as unnatural incursions while stairs (also accommodations) go unmarked 239\. **Author: Jill Gravink** 239, 240\.  
* **universal design** (for trails): Reimagining all trails as built interventions that can be conceived for diverse bodies 240-242.  
* **Scrambling, climbing, touching, holding: how to crip the trail map**  
* **queer ecology**: An ecological framework that sees beauty in the "wounds of the world" 242\. **Author: Catriona Sandilands** 242, 243\.  
* **embodied interdependence in nature**: Understanding nature through relationships with lovers, attendants, and service animals 233, 244, 245\. **Authors: Samuel Lurie** 245, 246, **Petra Kuppers** 233, 247, 248\.  
* **language as conversation with environment**: Constant wordless interaction with the physical environment as language 249, 250\. **Author: A.M. Baggs** 249, 251\.  
* **embodied environmentalism**: Artistic rendering showing humans as integral to ecosystems (e.g., carving bodies from trees) 252-254. **Author: Riva Lehrer** 252, 253, 255\.
